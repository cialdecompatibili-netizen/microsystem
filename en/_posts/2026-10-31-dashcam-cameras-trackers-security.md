A car, a home, a dog or a cat: security is not one device, it is a small system. Here is what each technology does, where people most often go wrong, and what we are preparing at MICROSYSTEM.

## Dashcams: the proof you need when something happens
A dashcam records the road while you drive and, with parking mode, also while the car is parked. What to look for:

- **Front and rear**: the most popular setup, because it covers both what is ahead and who hits you from behind.
- **A third channel inside the cabin**: useful for taxi and ride-share drivers, but you must think about passenger privacy.
- **A high-endurance SD card**: the card wears out because it writes constantly. A high-endurance card is a necessity, not a luxury.
- **4G connection**: lets you view and download footage remotely, handy for vans and tradespeople.

On privacy: personal use is normally allowed, but sharing videos and recording third parties on board has limits. Before using one for business, ask a consultant.

## Cameras for homes, building sites and places without WiFi
A country house, a building site, an olive grove or a boat often has neither WiFi nor mains power. These need a camera with a 4G SIM and a solar panel. Two things to clarify before buying:

- **SIM and data**: is it included, or do you pay separately?
- **Signal**: with weak coverage a good external antenna makes the difference. That is why, in our shop, cameras and pole antennas go together.

If the camera covers a street, neighbours or people, remember the privacy rules.

## Pets in the car: the risk people underestimate
A car in the sun heats up within minutes, and that can be dangerous for a dog or cat: never leave them in the car in hot weather, not even "just for a moment". A tracker or an interior camera does not replace a person being there, but they help in real cases: a pet that bolts from an open car, escapes the garden, or a cat that does not come home.

## Trackers: Bluetooth, WiFi, 4G or LoRa
There is no best tracker overall, only the right one for where your pet or vehicle lives.

- **Bluetooth** (AirTag-style tags): low cost, no subscription. It only works when a compatible phone is nearby, so it suits keys and bags better than a cat in the countryside.
- **WiFi**: alerts you when the pet leaves the home area. Outside coverage it sees nothing.
- **4G/LTE**: coverage almost anywhere there is a network, with real-time position. Downsides: it needs a SIM, many models require a subscription, and battery life is shorter.
- **LoRa / Meshtastic**: no SIM and no subscription, long range with low power use. Downsides: it works if there is a mesh of nodes or a repeater nearby, and in Europe the 868 MHz band has power limits.

Amazon US sales data (not Italy, so only an indication) shows that among the best-selling pet products are collar holders for AirTags, QR-code tags and GPS trackers with a subscription: people mostly look for simple, affordable solutions.
